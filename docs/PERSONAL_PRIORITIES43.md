# Personal interests in the existing daily4.3 controller

The20 direction IDs come from editorial.json's original hierarchy, including1–3,
6–18,21–24 and excluding19 as a compulsory topic. They are operational preferences,
not requirements for essays, lengths, issue size or publication.

`editorial_priorities43.json` contains bilingual explainable patterns, categories,
weekly compensation and a soft9-of12 preference. A candidate stores its group,
primary/additional directions, interest/source-quality/novelty/usefulness/diversity
scores. Publication/rights eligibility remains separate and unchanged. An already
started article resumes first. With alternatives,9 priority stories precede three
open places; without alternatives all legitimate stories remain available.

Search also uses personal interests and the7-day deficit, within14 days of public
archive history. Cached last checks and diversity of covered directions rotate
publishers. No extra Gemini classification, title rewriting or issue audit exists.
The fixed34 URLs are a reserve, not the source of catalogue discovery. New records
receive their actual thematic category rather than an unconditional Science label.

`editorial_sources.json` is the sole registry. publishers43.json is a compatibility
pointer, not a second dataset. Entries retain scientific journals and add the
owner's family, Moscow, travel, culture, architecture, movement, economics, law,
Christian history and engineering publishers. Different Nature journals are
separate editions but share one publisher group for variety preferences.

All new entries start disabled. The no-Secrets/no-Gemini public CI diagnostic
checks robots, the real homepage, advertised RSS/Atom and owned article links;
its metadata report enables **public discovery only**. It grants no copyright and
makes no assertion that every article is complete/open or scientifically sound.
Full originals still pass existing extraction, per-article rights and substantive
fact/safety/independence checks before use. Paywalls and restricted originals
remain blocked. No invented feed URLs or scanned archive mirrors are used.
Unavailable publishers remain manual references and cannot block release.
At most90 verified editions enter discovery; other registered references remain
available for future verification. Five publishers and eight full-source attempts
per run,6-hour cache and the existing shared quota/mutex are unchanged.

Archive articles are not rejected merely for being older than a year. Their true
publication date is preserved. The existing current-news/event-date gate remains
mandatory: archives are not current events, venue opening hours or current law.
Current Russian law needs official legal sources; medical/child advice keeps its
independent competent evidence requirement. Already verified relevant independent
sources may be reused from the protected checkpoint without inventing references.
A catalogue quality score is an editorial preference, never a factual approval.

`editorial_report43` stays in the existing encrypted checkpoint: prepared count,
priority counts/directions/groups,7-day absences, publishers, source languages and
science/category shares. Published count remains unknown until HTTPS delivery is
confirmed. This advisory report is never added to the public website/release JSON,
and failures to prepare it cannot veto publication. After HTTPS verification, the
existing monitor additionally builds an aggregate from already-public final pages
and retains it as a30-day Actions artifact. It reads no encrypted draft, calls no
Gemini, exports no article text, and is nonblocking. This supplies the actual
published composition even when the next producer has already moved to tomorrow.

12 remains a target; partial issues and the no-Gemini source digest,06:00 Moscow,
watchdog, translations,24/run and220/day, cooldown and Secrets are unchanged.
Historical HTML/Markdown/manifests are not rewritten.
